<p>Production-oriented research codebase for <strong>dynamic industrial job-shop scheduling</strong> with deep reinforcement learning. The system models a factory floor with machines, tray capacities, recipe compatibility constraints, and continuously arriving jobs, then trains policies that make <strong>direct job–machine assignment decisions</strong> to minimize tardiness.</p>
<blockquote>
<p><strong>Research write-up:</strong> <a href="http://urn.kb.se/resolve?urn=urn:nbn:se:uu:diva-538891">URN: urn:nbn:se:uu:diva-538891</a></p>
</blockquote>

<h2>Executive Summary</h2>
<p>Manufacturing plants continuously receive production orders that must be sequenced across specialized machines under hard constraints: recipe compatibility, tray capacity, process duration, and due dates. Classical dispatch rules (FIFO, EDD, handcrafted heuristics) are fast but myopic—they optimize local criteria and struggle when machine capabilities overlap and job mix shifts over time.</p>
<p>This repository implements a <strong>Gymnasium</strong> simulation of that decision process and trains <strong>Deep Q-Network (DQN)</strong>, <strong>Advantage Actor-Critic (A2C)</strong>, and <strong>Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO)</strong> agents with <strong>Stable-Baselines3</strong> / <strong>PyTorch</strong>. Instead of selecting a dispatch rule, the agent chooses an explicit <code>(job, machine)</code> pairing (or No-Op / start-machine action) from a structured observation of queues, capacities, deadlines, and machine state.</p>
<p><strong>Business impact targeted by the control objective</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>Fewer late jobs (lower mean tardiness)</li>
<li>Higher effective throughput under capacity constraints</li>
<li>Better utilization of specialized vs multi-purpose machines</li>
<li>A trainable policy that adapts as job mix and load change</li>
</ul>
<p>In the primary evaluation configuration (<strong>2 machines · 2 recipes · buffer size 3 · 1000 episodes</strong>), a trained DQN policy reduces late-job rate to <strong>~0.022%</strong> versus <strong>~0.13%</strong> for EDD and <strong>~0.51%</strong> for FIFO on the same evaluation artifacts.</p>

<h2>Why Reinforcement Learning?</h2>
<p>Job-shop scheduling is an NP-hard combinatorial optimization problem. In industrial settings it is also <strong>dynamic</strong>: new jobs arrive online, machines become free asynchronously, and feasible assignments depend on evolving capacity and recipe locks.</p>
<table>
<thead>
<tr>
<th>Classical approach</th>
<th>Limitation in this setting</th>
</tr>
</thead>
<tbody>
<tr>
<td>Exact MIP / CP solvers</td>
<td>Hard to re-solve at every event under tight latency</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>Metaheuristics (GA, ACO, PSO)</td>
<td>Expensive to warm-start continuously</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>Dispatch rules</td>
<td>Fast, but local and brittle under multi-skill machines</td>
</tr>
</tbody>
</table>
<p>RL is a natural fit because:</p>
<ol>
<li>The plant can be cast as an MDP with delayed rewards (completions happen after assignment).</li>
<li>Policies can be trained offline in simulation and executed with millisecond inference.</li>
<li>Direct action parameterization removes the need to hand-select a dispatch rule family.</li>
<li>Value-based methods (DQN) handle discrete constrained action sets well when exploration is managed carefully.</li>
</ol>

<p>Implemented in <a href="custom_environment/environment.py"><code>custom_environment/environment.py</code></a> as a Gymnasium <code>Env</code>.</p>
<h3>Decision process</h3>
<p>At each decision point the agent may:</p>
<ol>
<li><strong>Assign</strong> a pending (or uncompleted-buffer) job to an available machine, if recipe + tray constraints allow</li>
<li><strong>Start</strong> a machine that already has scheduled jobs</li>
<li><strong>No-Op</strong>, advancing simulated factory time to the next meaningful event</li>
</ol>

<h3>Action space</h3>
<p>[
|\mathcal{A}| = |\mathcal{M}| \cdot B + 1 + |\mathcal{M}|
]</p>
<p>(+ optional uncompleted-buffer assignment branch in expanded settings)</p>
<p>where (B) is buffer size and (+1) is No-Op.</p>
<h3>Reward shaping</h3>
<p>Dense step rewards encourage feasible scheduling and discourage myopic / illegal behavior:</p>
<table>
<thead>
<tr>
<th>Event</th>
<th align="right">Typical weight</th>
</tr>
</thead>
<tbody>
<tr>
<td>Job completed on time</td>
<td align="right"><code>+10</code></td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>Job completed late</td>
<td align="right"><code>-15</code></td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>Valid assignment</td>
<td align="right"><code>+1</code> to <code>+5</code></td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>Deadline pressure / idle misuse</td>
<td align="right"><code>-5</code> … <code>-20</code></td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>Invalid recipe / unavailable machine</td>
<td align="right"><code>-20</code> … <code>-30</code></td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>Illegal action</td>
<td align="right"><code>-15</code></td>
</tr>
</tbody>
</table>
<p>Rewards were iteratively refined against training curves (see research write-up, Section 3.6.3 / 4.x).</p>
<h3>Episode lifecycle &#x26; termination</h3>
<ul>
<li>Episodes run up to <code>max_steps</code> (commonly 4k–10k for eval, larger for train).</li>
<li>During training, catastrophic cumulative reward can trigger early termination (<code>is_evaluation=False</code>).</li>
<li>Factory clock advances either by 1 tick, by No-Op jump, or to the soonest machine completion.</li>
</ul>
<h3>Industrial constraints encoded</h3>
<ul>
<li>Recipe eligibility per machine (specialized vs multi-purpose)</li>
<li>Tray capacity packing before process start</li>
<li>Stochastic / distributional recipe arrivals inspired by plant recipe frequencies</li>
<li>Optional refresh of arrival time when jobs enter the buffer (<code>refresh_arrival_time</code>)</li>
</ul>

<h3>Policy network</h3>
<p>SB3 <code>MultiInputPolicy</code> flattens the Dict observation and feeds an MLP:</p>
<ul>
<li>Default DQN net: <code>[64, 64]</code> ReLU</li>
<li>Stronger DQN configs: <code>[128, 128, 64]</code></li>
<li>Discount (\gamma) typically <code>0.6–0.99</code> (higher (\gamma) for long-horizon tardiness)</li>
</ul>
<h3>Experience &#x26; optimization (DQN)</h3>
<ol>
<li>ε-greedy exploration with configurable <code>exploration_fraction</code></li>
<li>Replay buffer (e.g. 20k transitions), minibatch SGD on Bellman residual</li>
<li>Target network soft/hard updates via SB3 defaults</li>
<li>Periodic evaluation + <code>PlotTrainingCallback</code> for reward/tardiness curves</li>
</ol>
<h3>Training loop (conceptual)</h3>
<pre><code class="language-text">for t in 1..T:
  a_t ~ π_θ(s_t)            # or ε-greedy for DQN
  s_{t+1}, r_t, done = env.step(a_t)
  store / update
  if callback_interval: plot + checkpoint
</code></pre>

<h3>Baselines</h3>
<ul>
<li><strong>Random</strong> — uniform illegal-prone exploration reference</li>
<li><strong>FIFO</strong> — oldest pending job first</li>
<li><strong>EDD</strong> — earliest due date first</li>
<li><strong>Heuristic (HDR)</strong> — prioritize high process-time/deadline ratio + capacity-aware starts</li>
</ul>
<h3>Evaluation protocol</h3>
<ul>
<li>Deterministic policy rollout (<code>predict(..., deterministic=True)</code>)</li>
<li>Typically <strong>1000 episodes</strong></li>
<li>Metrics logged per episode: cumulative reward, tardiness %, jobs on time (JOT), jobs late (JNOT)</li>
<li>Results cached as pickles under <code>files/data/</code> for reproducible plotting (<code>compare_agents.py</code>)</li>
</ul>

<h2>How It Works</h2>
<ol>
<li><strong>Data / scenario loading</strong> — <code>init_custom_factory_env(...)</code> samples recipes from industrial frequency weights and builds machines with valid recipe sets.</li>
<li><strong>Environment creation</strong> — returns a Gymnasium-compatible <code>FactoryEnv</code> with Dict observations.</li>
<li><strong>Training</strong> — SB3 agent collects rollouts, optimizes policy/value parameters, checkpoints models, and emits learning curves via callback.</li>
<li><strong>Evaluation</strong> — frozen policy rolls out for (N) episodes; metrics persisted to <code>files/data/*.pkl</code>.</li>
<li><strong>Visualization</strong> — <code>compare_agents.py</code> and <code>scripts/generate_docs_assets.py</code> produce comparative charts and GIFs.</li>
<li><strong>Prediction / inference</strong> — <code>model.predict(obs, deterministic=True)</code> maps live observations to scheduling actions.</li>
</ol>

<p>Training hyperparameters (steps, γ, network width, machine/recipe counts) are defined in each agent’s <code>__main__</code> block. Checkpoints land in <code>files/trainedAgents/</code>.</p>

</code></pre>
<h3>Baseline + RL comparison</h3>
<pre><code class="language-bash">python compare_agents.py
</code></pre>
<p>Uses cached pickles when present; otherwise runs episodic evaluations and writes plots under <code>files/plots/</code>.</p>

<h2>Engineering Decisions</h2>
<table>
<thead>
<tr>
<th>Decision</th>
<th>Rationale</th>
<th>Tradeoff</th>
</tr>
</thead>
<tbody>
<tr>
<td><strong>Gymnasium</strong></td>
<td>Standard interface; SB3/env_checker ecosystem</td>
<td>Custom rendering still needed for factory UX</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td><strong>Stable-Baselines3</strong></td>
<td>Reliable DQN/A2C/PPO, Dict obs support via <code>MultiInputPolicy</code></td>
<td>Less research flexibility than a from-scratch trainer</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td><strong>DQN as primary algorithm</strong></td>
<td>Discrete constrained actions; strong empirical sample efficiency here</td>
<td>Sensitive to reward scale &#x26; exploration schedule</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td><strong>Direct job–machine actions</strong></td>
<td>Avoids dispatch-rule indirection; learns packing + timing jointly</td>
<td>Action space grows with (B \times M)</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td><strong>Dense shaped rewards</strong></td>
<td>Completions are delayed; shaping stabilizes early learning</td>
<td>Risk of proxy-objective mismatch vs pure tardiness</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td><strong>Iterative obs expansion</strong></td>
<td>Feature ablations (queue, availability, tray caps) drove gains</td>
<td>Longer experiment cycles; config sprawl in filenames</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td><strong>Pickle-cached eval</strong></td>
<td>Cheap replotting for 1000-episode studies</td>
<td>Must version filenames carefully per config</td>
</tr>
</tbody>
</table>
<h3>Scalability notes</h3>
<ul>
<li>Observation/action sizes scale with machines and buffer length.</li>
<li>Multi-million to hundreds-of-millions step runs were used for harder configs.</li>
<li>Larger plants likely need action masking, hierarchical policies, or graph encoders.</li>
</ul>
<hr>
<h2>Limitations</h2>
<ol>
<li><strong>Reward design</strong> — shaped rewards accelerate learning but can drift from pure business KPIs; some training phases show reward regressions.</li>
<li><strong>Observation completeness</strong> — residual information gaps remain for long-horizon contention.</li>
<li><strong>Generalization</strong> — policies are configuration-sensitive (machine map, recipe mix, arrival semantics).</li>
<li><strong>Compute</strong> — competitive DQN runs often require (10^7)–(10^8+) environment steps.</li>
<li><strong>Deployment gap</strong> — bridging simulation assumptions to live MES/APS integration (latency, uncertainty, human overrides) is future work.</li>
<li><strong>A2C/PPO</strong> — on-policy methods underperformed DQN on several setups in this MDP.</li>
</ol>
<hr>
<h2>Future Work</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Transformer / sequence policies</strong> for queue-aware attention over jobs</li>
<li><strong>Graph Neural Networks</strong> over job–machine bipartite graphs</li>
<li><strong>Multi-agent RL</strong> (machine-local actors + global critic)</li>
<li><strong>Curriculum learning</strong> from small plants → full recipe catalogs</li>
<li><strong>Action masking</strong> for illegal assignments (cleaner exploration)</li>
<li><strong>Distributed training</strong> with Ray RLlib / large-scale rollout workers</li>
<li><strong>Experiment tracking</strong> with Weights &#x26; Biases or MLflow</li>
<li><strong>Cloud deployment</strong> of inference microservices beside factory digital twins</li>
<li><strong>Safer reward learning</strong> / constrained RL for hard service-level objectives</li>
</ul>
<hr>
<h2>Research Contributions</h2>
<ol>
<li><strong>Direct assignment MDP</strong> for industrial batch/job-shop scheduling with tray capacities and recipe constraints—not merely dispatch-rule selection.</li>
<li><strong>Iterative environment/obs/reward engineering methodology</strong> showing which features (especially machine availability) unlock stable DQN learning.</li>
<li><strong>Systematic comparison</strong> of DQN vs A2C vs PPO vs FIFO/EDD/HDR across specialized and multi-purpose machine regimes.</li>
<li><strong>Evidence</strong> that value-based DRL can beat strong due-date heuristics on tardiness in the primary configuration, while exposing throughput–tardiness trade-offs in all-purpose settings.</li>
<li><strong>Reproducible research engineering artifacts</strong>: factories, callbacks, cached evaluations, and regeneration scripts for figures/GIFs.</li>
</ol>
